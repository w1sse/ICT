PRACTICAL SAFETY GUIDE

Recognise the warning signs.

Scammers may pretend to be bank employees, police officers, delivery services, relatives or investment experts. They often try to make you feel frightened, excited or rushed.

📞

Suspicious phone calls

A caller says your money is in danger or asks you to move it to a “safe account”.

  • End the call if you feel pressured.
  • Never install an app because a stranger asks you to.
  • Call the organisation using its official number.
✉️

Fake messages and phishing

A text or email asks you to open a link, sign in, pay a fee or confirm personal details.

  • Do not click unexpected links.
  • Check the sender and website address carefully.
  • Do not download unknown files.
💳

Payment and investment scams

An offer promises quick profit, asks for advance payment or says you must act now.

  • Be careful with guaranteed profits.
  • Verify the company independently.
  • Do not pay to claim an unexpected prize.
👪

Fake relative or friend

A message claims someone you know urgently needs money. AI can imitate voices and faces.

  • Contact the person through a different channel.
  • Verify before sending money.
🔗

Fake websites

A page may look almost identical to a real bank, shop or government service.

  • Type the official address yourself.
  • Look for misspellings and unusual domains.
  • A lock icon alone does not prove trustworthiness.
🏢

Fake support or officials

Someone claims to be support and asks for remote access or private information.

  • Do not give a stranger control of your device.
  • Contact the service through its official app or website.
